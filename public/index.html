    Try, compare, explain.

    Keep the article fixed and change the audience. Then keep the brief fixed and change the article. Compare actual outputs and verify each bullet against its source.

    01

    Write a useful role and goal

    Connect the agent's function to an audience and a concrete summary task. A role label alone is not proof of expertise.

    02

    Supply bounded source context

    The article is task data in a user message. It is not concatenated into the reusable instruction string.

    03

    Make constraints checkable

    Ask for exactly three bullets and define their length. Formatting checks are distinct from checking whether each bullet is supported.

    04

    Specify the output format

    A precise brief reduces ambiguity. The live result reports structural bullet checks without claiming factual correctness.

    05

    Use examples carefully

    The brief includes a short format example. The classroom article remains the evidence for the current summary.

    06

    Inspect the message boundary

    Changing article text should not rewrite the agent's instruction role. Role separation is inspectable structure, not a guarantee of model compliance.
